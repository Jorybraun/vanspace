# VanSpace sponsor tier pricing and value design

## Executive summary

VanSpace does not need to raise its top ask above CAD $10,000 to look credible. Public developer and community-event packages show $10,000 is a credible premium anchor: DjangoCon US lists Platinum at $10,000 and WordCamp Canada’s highest published tier is CAD $8,000.[1][2] These are directional benchmarks, not direct price comparables, but they establish that a $10,000 ceiling is not inherently too high.

The bigger issue is inventory design. VanSpace has a small room, a finite number of speaking opportunities, no sponsor tables, and an editorial standard we should protect. Selling every open session as a premium sponsor slot would weaken the exact trust the package is trying to sell. Comparable developer events often limit sponsor speaking or refuse to sell full speaking slots outright.[3][5]

My recommendation is:

1. Keep a **$10,000 Platinum / Anchor** tier, limited to one sponsor per event.
2. Keep a **$7,500 Gold / Session Partner** tier, limited to two editorially approved sessions per event.
3. Drop Silver as a standing tier, or turn it into a small number of named add-ons rather than another general package.
4. Keep a **$2,500 Supporter** tier with no stage access.
5. Sell the high-value extras separately: video/content, student access, speaker travel, and artist-designed tote/tee.
6. Keep the Season Partner outside the single-event table and price it as a negotiated multi-event agreement.

This gives VanSpace clearer gaps without pretending that a 150-person event has the inventory of a large conference.

## 1. What the market evidence says

### $10,000 is a defensible premium anchor

There is no single sponsorship market price. Published packages range from small community tiers to larger conference packages. DevOpsDays Wollongong, which describes a 150–200-person technical audience, publishes AUD $12,000 Platinum, $8,000 Gold, $5,000 Silver, $2,500 Bronze, and $1,000 Startup.[3] WordCamp Canada publishes CAD $8,000, $5,000, $2,000, and $700 tiers.[2]

More established developer events sit higher. DjangoCon US publishes Diamond at $20,000, Platinum at $10,000, Gold at $6,000, Silver at $3,500, and Bronze at $2,300.[1]

The useful conclusion is not that VanSpace should copy one of these tables. It is that a $10,000 top tier can be credible if the product is scarce and specific: a premium content moment, association with the day, a clear audience, and a defensible community contribution.

### Three to five tiers are common, but tier count is not the value

DjangoCon and CppCon use multiple standard levels, while CopyCon uses four clearly differentiated packages and separate bespoke add-ons.[1][4][5] That does not mean VanSpace needs four paid tiers. A small event benefits from fewer choices when the inventory is limited. The important question is whether a sponsor can immediately see why it should move up one level.

The current VanSpace prices—$10,000, $7,500, $5,000, and $2,500—create ratios of approximately 1.33×, 1.5×, and 2× between adjacent tiers. Those gaps are not automatically wrong, but they demand very clear differences in stage access, scarcity, content distribution, and community impact. If two adjacent tiers both receive “visibility” and “some speaking,” the lower tier will cannibalize the higher one.

A practical buyer-side rule is to make the date, location, audience, and sponsor benefit visible immediately; missing basics reduce the chance that a sponsor investigates further.[6]

### Scarcity is a real value signal

Public packages repeatedly state availability limits: WordCamp Canada describes its highest tiers as one of three and one of six.[2] Scarcity works here because it is attached to a specific asset, not because the package merely says “limited time.”

For VanSpace, the scarce assets are not booths. They are:

- One premium underwriter per event
- A small number of editorially approved sponsor sessions
- The recording/distribution package
- The artist-designed tote/tee partnership
- Student or community access support
- Speaker travel and hospitality support

Those should be visibly limited in the deck.

## 2. The important editorial lesson

VanSpace should not promise “pay for a keynote” in a way that makes the event feel purchased.

CopyCon says “Sponsorship buys association, not airtime,” and any sponsor spotlight is ring-fenced from the keynote programme.[5] DevOpsDays Wollongong says it does not offer full speaker slots in return for sponsorship, although sponsors can submit talks.[3]

That is the direction VanSpace’s package should make explicit. The package should say:

> Sponsor-led talks are editorially reviewed. A product can be part of the story, but the story must be useful to builders.

A sponsor can bring a real technical lesson, product-building story, demo with educational value, or fireside conversation. They cannot simply buy a microphone and turn the room into a pitch deck.

This is not only an audience-protection rule. It increases sponsor value: the sponsor is associated with a good piece of content instead of being marked as an interruption.

## 3. Recommended VanSpace structure

### Platinum / Anchor — CAD $10,000

**One per event.**

**Job:** underwrite the production and take the most substantial sponsor contribution.

**Include:**

- One premium stage moment, approximately 30–40 minutes, subject to editorial approval
- Optional panel or moderated conversation if the format exists
- Top sponsor placement on the event page and sponsor materials
- Association with the recording package, subject to speaker and production permissions
- Artist-designed tote/tee association, if the creative partnership is selected
- 8–10 tickets
- A post-event report with attendance, content outputs, and social/video links
- Optional category exclusivity written into the agreement

**Do not promise:** replacing Kent or Wes, uncontrolled editorial influence, attendee email lists, or unlimited stage time.

### Gold / Session Partner — CAD $7,500

**Two per event, maximum.**

**Job:** contribute a useful, editorially approved session.

**Include:**

- One 20–25 minute talk, demo, or fireside conversation
- Topic and speaker approved through a lightweight editorial review
- Website sponsor placement and link
- Inclusion in relevant lineup/social announcements
- Video/social association where the session is recorded and cleared
- One useful bag insert or tool/resource contribution
- 5–6 tickets
- A short post-event summary

The price is close enough to Platinum to be plausible, but the difference must be obvious: Platinum underwrites the day and owns the largest sponsor moment; Gold owns one useful session.

### Supporter — CAD $2,500

**Four to six per event.**

**Job:** support access and be associated with the community without buying stage time.

**Include:**

- Website sponsor listing and link
- Sponsor recognition in the shared thank-you treatment
- One approved bag insert, tool credit, or community resource
- 2–3 tickets
- Inclusion in the post-event sponsor list

**No stage. No implied product pitch.** This protects the Gold product and keeps the room from becoming sponsor-heavy.

### What to do with Silver

I would not keep Silver as a general-purpose tier. It sits between Gold and Supporter without a naturally distinct job.

If you want to preserve the $5,000 price point, turn it into a named add-on rather than a tier:

- **Video Partner — $5,000:** sponsor recognition in the recording package, website archive, and agreed social assets.
- **Student Access Partner — $5,000:** underwrite student/community tickets and receive a clear impact report.
- **Speaker Support Partner — $5,000:** contribute to travel and hospitality, with recognition in the event and recap.
- **Artist Edition Partner — $5,000 plus production:** support the local artist tote/tee run, with a limited, tasteful mark.

This gives a buyer control without weakening the main ladder.

## 4. What makes the package worth their time

### A. Content with a long tail

CppCon explicitly separates video sponsorship from ordinary event sponsorship because videos reach people who could not attend and remain online indefinitely.[4] DjangoCon offers logo placement on all conference talk videos available forever.[1]

VanSpace should not promise “nationwide reach” before it has data. It can promise a planned content workflow:

- Record the talk where permissions allow
- Publish the full video when edited and cleared
- Add sponsor recognition to the agreed video treatment
- Publish short clips or quotes socially
- Link the sponsor and relevant resources in the description
- Include the output in the post-event report

The deck should label this **planned distribution**, not guaranteed reach.

### B. Student and community access

DjangoCon says sponsorship funds grants, speaker stipends, accessibility, meals, and care.[1] CppCon offers a scholarship sponsorship that covers registration, transportation, food, and lodging for students.[4] This is a stronger story than generic “brand exposure.”

VanSpace can offer a student-access or hackathon-tools partnership tied to a concrete number:

- Number of subsidized tickets
- Software credits or API access provided
- Number of schools or groups reached
- A short post-event impact note

### C. Product in context, without a booth

VanSpace has already decided that tables are not the right product. That is defensible: DevOpsDays warns that noisy vendor marketing can work against sponsor credibility and recommends engineers, solutions engineers, and developer advocates connecting with the community on its terms.[3]

Replace the booth with:

- An approved educational demo
- A short technical clinic or Q&A
- Tool credits used in the hackathon
- A product resource in the bag
- A useful technical guide or open-source contribution
- A sponsor-supported community or student moment

### D. Artist-designed physical goods

Swag is strongest when it has a reason to exist. DjangoCon and WordCamp Canada use bag, T-shirt, and physical recognition as explicit sponsor inventory.[1][3]

VanSpace’s better version is a limited artist-designed tote and T-shirt. Sell the association with the object, not a wall of logos:

> A local artist makes the edition. A sponsor helps make it possible. The object is something people want to keep.

Do not promise the design before it exists. Offer the partnership as a limited creative opportunity with approval over the final treatment.

### E. Reporting that is useful but honest

A post-event report should include:

- Attendance and ticket mix
- Sessions recorded and published
- Video links and views at report date
- Social posts and engagement at report date
- Sponsor assets delivered
- Student/community support delivered
- Photos and selected quotes

Do not promise a fixed number of views or “thousands reached.” CopyCon’s public prospectus is a good model for honest audience language: it describes the format and audience without overclaiming geography or total reach.[5]

## 5. Season Partner structure

Do not bury the Season Partner inside the $10,000 single-event table.

A season package should be treated as its own product rather than quietly included in a single-event package. The exact price should be negotiated from the number of events, rights, and reporting commitments.

For VanSpace, define the Season Partner separately:

- Covers the September hackathon and November VanSpace event
- Receives a combined content and recognition plan
- Gets priority on the artist edition and student-access story
- Receives one combined post-season report
- Is priced by agreement, above a single-event Platinum

If the $10,000 ceiling applies to each event, a season package can reasonably be negotiated above that ceiling. If $10,000 is the maximum total cheque, then it should not be called a full-season underwrite; it should be a “Season Supporter” with narrower rights.

## 6. Proposed gaps

### Recommended default ladder

| Tier | Price | Slots | Core product |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Platinum / Anchor | $10,000 | 1 | Major educational stage moment + production underwrite + highest content/impact association |
| Gold / Session Partner | $7,500 | 2 | Approved 20–25 minute talk, demo, or fireside + content association |
| Supporter | $2,500 | 4–6 | Community support + website/link + approved resource or bag insert, no stage |

### Add-ons

| Add-on | Suggested price | Scarcity |
|---|---:|---:|
| Video / content partner | $5,000 | 1 |
| Student access partner | $5,000 | 1 |
| Speaker support partner | $5,000 | 1 |
| Artist edition partner | $5,000 + production | 1 |
| Tool/API credits | In-kind or cash equivalent | 1–2 |

These figures are recommendations, not market facts. They should be validated with sponsor conversations and cost estimates.

## 7. What I would change in the deck

1. Replace “7 premium speaking slots open” with **“Two Gold session slots open”** plus **“Platinum anchor available.”** Seven total sessions can remain in the programme, but they should not all look like paid sponsor inventory.
2. Keep the $10,000 Platinum ceiling, but make it one-event and scarce.
3. Keep Gold at $7,500 only if it has a clearly editorial, 20–25 minute session product. Otherwise test $6,000.
4. Remove Silver from the standard ladder and convert it to add-ons.
5. Rename Bronze to Supporter if the goal is community association rather than a traditional sponsor hierarchy.
6. Add a one-line editorial guardrail to every stage tier.
7. Add an explicit “what happens after the event” row: recording, social clips, links, photos, and report.
8. Keep the Season Partner on its own page and price it separately.

## Open questions

- Is September 10, September 12, or October 10 the correct hackathon date? Current project materials conflict.
- Does the $10,000 ceiling apply to one event or to the entire fall season?
- Will VanSpace definitely record and publish sessions, and what speaker permissions are required?
- How many paid sponsor-led sessions can the programme support without changing the community character?
- What are the actual costs for speaker travel, venue, food, AV, recording, tickets, and artist merch?
- Is the scheduler URL available, or should the deck continue to use email only?
