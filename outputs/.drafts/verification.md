**1. Citation [2] (PyOhio 2026) cannot be verified - PDF source inaccessible**

The draft makes several critical claims about PyOhio 2026 that cannot be verified because the source is a PDF:
- "PyOhio lists Platinum at $10,000" (line 5)
- "PyOhio lists one Premier and two Platinum slots" (line 40)
- "PyOhio publishes Premier at $15,000, Platinum at $10,000, Gold at $7,500, Silver at $5,000, and Bronze at $2,500" (line 26)
- "PyOhio states that sponsored talks go through the same quality review as other talks and that irrelevant content will be rejected" (line 57)

These claims are used as primary evidence for:
- Establishing that $10,000 is a defensible premium anchor price
- Demonstrating that scarcity signals value (one Premier, two Platinum slots)
- Supporting the argument for editorial review of sponsor talks

**Why this is MAJOR:** PyOhio is one of three key benchmarks used to justify the $10,000 ceiling. Without verifiable evidence from this source, the benchmarking argument relies on only two verified sources (DjangoCon and WordCamp Canada). The editorial review policy claim is also central to the draft's recommendations about protecting content quality.

**How to fix:** Either:
- Replace the PyOhio PDF citation with a web-accessible source (e.g., an HTML sponsorship page), or
- Find an alternative web-verifiable developer event with similar pricing to use as a third benchmark, or
- Remove the PyOhio-specific claims and rely on the two verified sources (DjangoCon and WordCamp Canada) with acknowledgment that the benchmarking set is smaller
